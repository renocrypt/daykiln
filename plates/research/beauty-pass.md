## Materials

Judged from publisher previews: Fab gallery images at 2,560 px, Poliigon previews at 900 px including their "100% zoom" crops, and Substance detail renders. Texel density in px/cm is our arithmetic from the stated resolution and physical size; it sets how close a macro camera can come before the surface softens. Megascans on Fab cost $0.99 (Personal) or $9.99 (Professional) per asset unless noted. Poliigon premium assets need a subscription: Unlimited is $27 a month billed yearly or $54 billed monthly; Business Unlimited is $59 or $119 ([pricing](https://www.poliigon.com/pricing)).

**Consequence.** Every material below that clears the beauty bar comes from a library that forbids committing raw files to a public repository. The public repository could hold CC0 materials, procedural generators, and build scripts; paid masters would need a private store. Fab does not say whether a public three.js site that serves compressed textures meets "restrict end users from extracting". Poliigon's and Textures.com's terms appear to rule out an open-source piece entirely. Decide this, or get written confirmation from the library, before buying a paid material for shipping. Fab's share-alike clause also matters for UNFOLD: the Rumsey Coronelli gores are CC BY-NC-SA, so baking them with a Megascans paper into one texture may conflict; SLNSW files would not (see [Full-resolution access](#full-resolution-access)).

### Brass

No library sells a true photoscan of brass or bronze today. Megascans "Brass" (`se4nbarc`, `scoacdjp`) and "Bronze" (`se4pcbbc`) appear on Fab's [List of Surfaces – Megascans Moving to Fab Later](https://support.fab.com/s/article/List-of-Surfaces-Megascans-Moving-to-Fab-Later?language=en_US) (November 12, 2025), and a Fab search returned neither. Poliigon's newest metal series (IDs 133xx) states "Creation Method: Hybrid AI — Created partially with generative image technology". Its older 7xxx and 3xxx metals state no creation method, except 7248, "Procedural". CGAxis states "Human-made by 3D artists. No AI generation." CGAxis plans run from $16 a month ($190 a year) to $41 a month ($490 a year), and an 8K texture costs 3 credits.

## Gaps

**Blockers for a public repository**

- **Every paid material pick.** Fab, Poliigon, Adobe, and Textures.com all forbid committing raw files publicly. Poliigon and Textures.com also appear to exclude open-source works outright. Fab requires shipped projects to "restrict end users from extracting" content and does not say whether a public web page serving compressed textures meets that. Decide on a private asset store for paid masters, or ask the libraries in writing, before buying for shipping.
- **Rumsey share-alike and Fab.** The Rumsey Coronelli files are CC BY-NC-SA; Fab forbids combining its content with share-alike content. SLNSW's own files would avoid both the non-commercial term and this conflict.
- **Marey and Orsay images.** The Cinémathèque française, the Collège de France, and GrandPalaisRmnPhoto all require written authorization for public use. Sending the requests is the user's decision.

**Not found**

- **Laid or chain lines** in any 3D texture library: Megascans, Poliigon, Textures.com, Substance, CGAxis, Texture Crate, or Poly Haven. Pixelbuddha lists a "handmade and laid paper" category of flat scans; only its landing page was opened, so its price, resolution, and license are unverified.
- **A scan of real 17th-century rag paper.** A custom photometric-stereo capture of a blank antique flyleaf is the only route to period-true paper.
- **A true photoscan of brass or bronze.** Megascans "Brass" and "Bronze" are listed as moving to Fab later, with no date.

**Caveats**

- Beauty judgements come from publisher previews. Look-development renders in the real renderer, with each direction's palette, tone mapping, and depth of field, decide final picks.
- Texel densities are our arithmetic from stated sizes. Tile scale is a look-development decision; physically true scale favours the 20–50 cm Poliigon tiles over 2–2.5 m Megascans tiles for the closest shots.
