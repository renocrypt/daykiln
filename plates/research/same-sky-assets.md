# SAME SKY: asset research

Researched on 2026-09-23. This is a catalogue only; nothing was downloaded.

> **2026-09-24:** The Role column records license status from this first pass. It does not say what is picked: selection is now beauty-first, and current picks are in [Selection in DIRECTIONS.md](../DIRECTIONS.md#selection).

Roles: **in the piece** means the license permits shipping the asset in a public web experience. **Reference only** means it informs the design but is never shipped. Licenses are quoted as the source states them. Sizes are the source's own figures, or "not stated".

## Top picks

1. **Sky model:** Bruneton's *Precomputed Atmospheric Scattering*, run in Three.js through `@takram/three-atmosphere` (MIT; the model code is BSD-3-Clause). It models ozone, which keeps the twilight zenith blue, and it accepts suns below the horizon. Bake the aperture's zenith color sequence offline so the probe reads fixed, reproducible values.
2. **Measured dusk sky:** Poly Haven *Qwantani Dusk 1* and *Dusk 2 (Pure Sky)* (CC0, 24K, unclipped, 12 EV). They are two measured frames of the same evening, 19 minutes apart. Check the sequence against the measured twilight spectra of Spitschan et al. 2016 (MIT raw data; CC BY 4.0 tables).
3. **Color appearance model:** Color.js CAM16 (MIT), whose viewing environment can be configured. It models the surround's luminance only, so it cannot predict a hue shift from a colored ceiling on its own. Key literature: Katz on film color, Ekroll & Faul on the size and locality of simultaneous contrast measured on monitors, and Pei, Farnand & Fairchild 2025 on extending CIECAM16.
4. **Skyspace reference:** *Twilight Epiphany* (Rice University, 2012; reopened 2026-09-15). It is the best-documented example of a knife-edge roof, lit ceiling, and daily sunrise and sunset sequences. Reference only.
5. **Albers:** Yale University Press's *Interaction of Color* Complete Digital Edition (2022, subscription, © Yale University). The 2013 iPad app is discontinued. Reference only. Plates need permission from Yale University Press and the Josef and Anni Albers Foundation.

**Limit of all four.** CIECAM02 and CAM16 take the background as a single luminance value (Color.js `backgroundLuminance`; Material `backgroundLstar`). They can predict how a bright ceiling darkens the sky. They cannot predict how a *colored* ceiling shifts the sky's hue, which is the effect SAME SKY stages. Pei, Farnand & Fairchild (2025) state that existing color appearance models are "inadequate" for simultaneous contrast and extend CIECAM16 to cover it. No public code was found. Any hue-shift estimate in the piece must therefore be labeled an approximation. One candidate is Ekroll & Faul's von Kries-plus-crispening model (below).

**What the evidence implies for a screen** (inference from the studies above, all run on monitors):

- The effect is measurable on ordinary displays with small fields of a few degrees.
- The ceiling band within about 1° of the aperture does most of the work, so the edge must meet the sky with no dark reveal. A gap under 1° nearly abolishes the effect. The knife edge is perceptually load-bearing, not only elegant.
- The part of the effect that comes from adaptation over time, rather than the local spatial part, is likely weaker on screens, because the viewer's own room stays in view.
- The effect peaks when the ceiling approaches the sky's own luminance and color, as it does at dusk.
- Viewers differ by up to about five times, so show the emitted value and let visitors judge the effect for themselves.

## Gaps

- **No open, measured zenith-sky radiance series through twilight was found.** Lee's midlatitude zenith spectra exist only in papers. The Granada skylight data needs permission for commercial use and appears to be daytime. Kider 2014 is daytime only. The Laval HDR sky database has restrictive terms. Decision: build sequences from a model (Bruneton or Prague), anchor them to the Qwantani HDR frames and the Spitschan spectra, and label them "modeled".
- **No JavaScript model of hue shift from a colored surround.** CAM16 handles surround luminance only. The Pei et al. 2025 extension has no public code. Decision: ship CAM16 estimates for lightness and chroma, and label any hue estimate an approximation, for example Ekroll & Faul's von Kries-plus-crispening model. Consider letting visitors match the sky against a neutral reference to record their own percept.
- **No peer-reviewed measurement inside a Skyspace** of how large the effect is.
- **Prague model:** no WebGL port, and the sun stops at −4.2°. Bruneton's demo textures total about 17 MB (16 MB of it scattering). Generate at runtime or bake a small table.
- **No public section drawing of a knife-edge detail** was found. The thickness figure (about 1/16 inch) comes from one newspaper report.
- **Rights:** Turrell imagery and Albers plates are all rights reserved. The rights status of Katz's 1935 translation is not stated on archive.org. The Poly Haven sun elevations are estimates from API timestamps read as local time; verify before quoting them.
- **Rice timing discrepancy:** the sunset start is "about 10 minutes" before sunset in the Thresher (2026) and "8–10" on the Moody page. Recheck before citing.
