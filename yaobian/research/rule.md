## Seen

- **The hall** is square below. In each corner a squinch hangs as a cascade of long pendants up to the octagon; over the middle of each wall a smaller cascade hangs between them, above a lattice window, over a doorway under a lobed arch.
- **The walls** are fields of fine geometric carving framed by borders and inscription bands, every surface covered; the dado ends in a row of black triangles on white.
- **The octagon's frieze** carries round bosses. **The drum's windows** stand in pairs between colonnettes, under stepped heads, with lattice screens: sixteen windows, the only daylight in the dome.
- **The dome** is thousands of small cells: at the spring a cell is perhaps a hand wide, and they shrink toward the apex. Sixteenfold and eightfold symmetry, a star at the apex. Rhythm from larger, deeper cells: rings of dark cavities, sixteen at mid-height and eight lower, pendants hanging in them.
- **The material** is cream-white plaster, with worn traces of blue, green, and red-brown paint on some cells.
- **The light** is soft: bright near the windows, each cell's hollow a small shadow, no shaft of sun inside the dome.

## Read

Jones and Goury's Plate X text: the pendentives of the Sala de la Barca are "composed of numerous prisms of plaster, united by their contiguous lateral surfaces, consisting of seven different forms, proceeding from three primary figures on plan": the right-angled triangle, the rectangle, and the isosceles triangle with a 45° apex. "The curves of the several pieces are similar." "Nearly five thousand pieces enter into the construction of the ceiling of the Hall of the Two Sisters." The Two Sisters' plan on the same plate is drawn in halves, pieces black, some white; the section is a stepped cone.

Scholarship to check the model against: Gámiz Gordo and Ferrer Pérez-Blanco, "A Grammar of Muqarnas: Drawings of the Alhambra by Jones and Goury (1834–1845)", *VLC arquitectura* 6, no. 2 (2019), [doi:10.4995/vlc.2019.10947](https://doi.org/10.4995/vlc.2019.10947); Ferrer-Pérez-Blanco, Gámiz-Gordo, and Reinoso-Gordo, "New Drawings of the Alhambra: Deformations of Muqarnas in the Pendentives of the Sala de la Barca", *Sustainability* 11, no. 2 (2019), [doi:10.3390/su11020316](https://doi.org/10.3390/SU11020316); Jean-Marc Castéra, "The Muqarnas Dome of the Hall of the Two Sisters in the Alhambra in Granada", in *Mathematics and Culture V* (Springer, 2007). *Verify* the piece count, the reading of the white pieces as cavities, and the hall's proportions against Castéra.

## Claims in RULE's public copy

- **"The Hall of the Two Sisters, in the Alhambra at Granada, built for the Nasrid sultan Muhammad V in the fourteenth century."** Checked 2026-09-24. The hall is on the north side of the Court of the Lions, the Palace of the Lions built under Muhammad V; the hall's own date is argued, from 1362–1365 after his return to the throne (Felix Arnold, cited by [Court of the Lions, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_the_Lions)) to his first reign, 1354–1359, so the copy gives the century. Its name is from the two twin marble slabs in its floor ([Patronato de la Alhambra y Generalife](https://www.alhambra-patronato.es/en/edificios-lugares/the-hall-of-the-two-sisters)).
- **"As Owen Jones and Jules Goury measured it and published it in 1842."** Their volume 1, above; Goury died in Granada in 1834, and the drawings are dated 1834 to 1845 by Gámiz Gordo and Ferrer Pérez-Blanco.
